The claim
 
The work did not change. The stakes did.
Nothing in Google's guide asks for anything new. What it asks for is the thing most sites were already skipping.
 
Every week somebody sells a small business an AI search package. It usually comes with a new file to upload and a new kind of markup to install. Google's own guide, published this quarter, says neither of those exists.
 
You do not need machine readable files, AI text files, markup or Markdown to appear in generative AI features. That is Google, in its own documentation.
Search Engine Journal read the same guide and put it plainly: the industry's new labels, AEO and GEO, are still SEO. The features are built on the core search ranking and quality systems, and they pull from the same index.
 
What is soldWhat Google says
An llms.txt or AI text fileNot needed. Does nothing.
A special AI schemaThere is no such markup.
A separate AI optimisationThe SEO best practices still apply.
A shortcut into the answerSame index, same ranking systems.
Which is worse news than it sounds, and better news than it sounds. Worse, because there is no button to press. Better, because the thing that does work is a page that answers a real question clearly, and that is something you can actually build.

The twenty minute check
About twenty minutes.
 
1
Ask your agency what they added
If the answer is a file or a markup, ask them to show you Google's page that asks for it.
 
2
Open your best page and read the first paragraph
If it does not answer the question in the title, that is the work, and it is not an AI problem.
 
3
Check you are in the index at all
Search Console tells you. Nothing appears in an AI answer that is not in the ordinary index first.
There is no trick underneath any of this. There is only the page, and whether it is worth reading.
